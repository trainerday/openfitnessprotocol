# The names "Open Fitness Protocol" and "OFP"

The code, the tables and the documents in this repository are free for anyone to use, change and ship
under the [Apache-2.0 license](LICENSE). The **names** are a separate matter: the license says so itself
(section 6: it grants no permission to use the licensor's trade names or trademarks).

**"Open Fitness Protocol"™ and "OFP"™ are trademarks of TrainerDay Inc.** They are unregistered marks,
claimed from the first public use of the names.

The names are protected for one reason: so that "works with OFP" means the same thing wherever a rider
reads it. Nobody is charged for using them, and nobody will be.

## What you may do, without asking

- **Say what is true.** "Reads devices through Open Fitness Protocol", "works with OFP", "an OFP table
  for the <device>", "built on OFP": in your app, on your box, in your documentation, in an article.
- **Call your app or device "OFP compatible"** when it reads devices with the unmodified OFP core from
  this repository, or with an implementation that decodes every recording in the registry to the same
  values as the core does. That is what this repository's own check verifies (`ofp check`, `npm test`); a
  separate, named conformance suite is planned and will take its place here when it exists.
- **Name the project** when you write about it, teach it, or contribute to it.
- **Fork the repository.** That is what the license is for.

## What you may not do

- **Present a changed version as the official one.** A fork that reads devices differently, has its own
  registry, or changes the table format takes its own name. "Based on Open Fitness Protocol" is fine;
  "Open Fitness Protocol" as the name of your version is not.
- **Say "OFP compatible"** of something that does not meet the line above.
- **Use the names as your own**: in a company name, a product name, a domain name or an account name, or
  in a logo, in a way that suggests the project is yours or that TrainerDay or the OFP project stands
  behind your product.
- **Register** the names, or names that would be confused with them, as trademarks or domains.

If you are not sure, ask: open an issue in this repository.

## Where this is going

TrainerDay started OFP and does not intend to own it forever. Once the project has contributors and other
apps using it, its stewardship is meant to pass to the community or to a neutral body, **and the names go
with it**. Holding the trademarks until then is how they stay in one piece to be handed over.
